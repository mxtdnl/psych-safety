    What you were actually spending

    You chose between responses, not between behaviours. Every response carried a hidden weighting across four leadership styles the survey measured — three points a round, twenty-four across the game. This is where yours went.

    Support

    Supportive leadership

    Concern for team members’ welfare, building mutual support. It affects psychological safety only indirectly — through team climate. In the model you just ran, the supportive weight never touched Safety directly. It moved Climate, and Climate pulled Safety after it.
    Indirect effect only · McKinsey, 2021
    Consult

    Consultative leadership

    Asking opinions before deciding, granting autonomy, seeking consensus. It works both directly and through climate — the only behaviour here that does both, and the only one carrying weight in all three of your meters.
    Standardised coefficient 0.54 · McKinsey, 2021
    Challenge

    Challenging leadership

    Asking people to re-examine assumptions and exceed what they thought possible. It can strengthen psychological safety — but only where a positive climate already exists. Below the line it did damage. The sequence is the finding.
    Conditional on positive climate · McKinsey, 2021
    Direct

    Authoritative leadership

    Command and control. Detrimental to psychological safety. It bought delivery early and less of it each round after that, while taking climate and safety down with it.
    Detrimental · McKinsey, 2021

    Solo mode

    Read the room

    Three parts, about twelve minutes. There is no feedback until the end.

    1. What you say you do. Twelve statements. How often is this actually true of you.
    2. What you judge. Eight situations. Rate all four responses for effectiveness — not one, all four.
    3. What you choose. Six decisions in which the considerate option costs you something real.
    Before you start
    This is a teaching instrument, not a validated psychometric. It has no norms, no reliability data and no predictive claim. The scoring key is derived from a single published survey and is shown to you in full at the end, so you can argue with it.
